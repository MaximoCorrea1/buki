Books stand face out, the way they do in a shop.

It reads the picture, not the caption

Right-click any cover image and Buki sends the photo and the words around it to a vision model together, because the text beside a cover is often what makes a hard one legible. Then it checks the answer against OpenLibrary rather than trusting it.

One photo can hold several books

A stack on a desk, a shelf behind someone's head. Each book it can read gets its own row and its own decision, or you take the lot at once.

It remembers why you wanted it

Every book keeps the page you caught it from. In March, a list of titles is just a list. The post that sold you on one is the reason you open it.

Every book scanner assumes the book is in your hands.

Buki is for the ones you will never hold. A cover in a post, a spine in a video thumbnail, a paperback on someone's desk in a photograph. If it is a picture in a browser, it is a book you can keep.

Your shelf never leaves your computer.

When you ask Buki to read a cover, the picture goes to Buki and on to the vision model, and the title it finds goes to OpenLibrary to confirm the book is real. That is everything that leaves. There is no account, no tracking, no analytics, and the shelf itself is stored in your browser and is never sent anywhere. Read the privacy policy.

Affiliate disclosure. Saved books carry a Buy link. As an Amazon Associate, Buki earns from qualifying purchases, at no extra cost to you. The link appears only on books you have already chosen to save, and it never changes which books Buki shows you or in what order. You can point it at Bookshop.org instead, which supports independent bookshops.

The questions worth answering.

Google Lens already does this, free.

Lens tells you what the book is. It does not keep it. Close the panel and the answer is gone, with nowhere it went and no record you wanted it. Buki is the shelf, not the answer.

Why pay when the source is open?

You never have to. The code is MIT and the free path is real: bring your own recognition key and read as many covers as you like, forever. Pro is for people who would rather not hold a key at all.

What happens if I stop paying?

Every book you caught stays on your shelf, opens, moves between piles and keeps its Buy link. Your shelf is on your computer and it is yours. Only new cover reading stops.

Will it get the book wrong?

Sometimes, which is why nothing is ever saved without you choosing a pile. The shelf also shows how often it was right, so you can see for yourself how much to trust it rather than taking our word.
